# AI Fluency for Product Managers

## Overview

AI fluency is the ability to work with artificial intelligence **effectively, efficiently, ethically, and safely**. For Product Managers, AI fluency goes beyond knowing how to use an AI tool. It requires understanding where AI creates product value, recognizing its limitations, evaluating its outputs, and maintaining appropriate human accountability.

## Why AI Fluency Matters in Product Management

Product Managers routinely work with large amounts of information, including customer needs, requirements, workflows, product data, technical constraints, and stakeholder feedback.

Generative AI can accelerate activities such as information synthesis, data organization, requirements analysis, brainstorming, and product discovery. However, AI-generated information can also be incomplete, inaccurate, unsupported, or inconsistent with the intended business context.

Therefore, AI should be used to **augment product judgment rather than replace it**.

A Product Manager remains responsible for determining whether an AI-assisted output is appropriate for the product, customer, and business environment.

## The 4D AI Fluency Framework

I use four concepts to structure responsible interaction with AI:

**Delegation: What should AI do?**  
Determine which tasks are appropriate to delegate to AI and which decisions should remain human-owned.

**Description: What exactly should AI do?**  
Provide sufficient context, objectives, constraints, source information, and expected output characteristics.

**Discernment: Is the output good enough?**  
Evaluate AI-generated results for qualities such as accuracy, relevance, groundedness, completeness, clarity, and safety.

**Diligence: Are we using AI responsibly?**  
Maintain appropriate human accountability, transparency, privacy, governance, monitoring, and escalation mechanisms.

## Product Decision Principle

AI should not be introduced simply because a capability can use AI.

My product evaluation starts with:

> **What user or business problem are we trying to solve, and is AI the appropriate mechanism for solving it?**

The decision should then consider the desired outcome, alternative solutions, AI capabilities and limitations, implementation risk, and the level of human oversight required.

## Applied Example: CareBridge AI

I am applying these principles to **CareBridge AI**, a product concept focused on helping recently discharged patients better understand and manage clinician-approved post-discharge instructions.

Potential AI capabilities include simplifying complex instructions, organizing information into actionable steps, translating approved content, and answering questions grounded in authorized source material.

AI would not independently diagnose conditions, prescribe treatment, modify medication instructions, or alter a clinician-approved care plan.

When the system cannot produce an appropriately grounded response, or encounters information requiring clinical judgment, the experience should transition to an approved human escalation path.

## Human Accountability

AI can assist with analysis, organization, explanation, and generation, but responsibility for product decisions remains with people.

For high-risk applications, particularly healthcare, the level of AI autonomy should decrease as the potential consequence of an incorrect output increases.

This leads to a principle I will continue applying throughout this portfolio:

> **Use AI where it creates meaningful value, constrain it where uncertainty creates risk, and preserve human authority where consequences require judgment.**

## AI Tools & Transparency

AI tools, including ChatGPT and Gemini, may be used throughout this portfolio to support brainstorming, research synthesis, critique, content refinement, and prototype experimentation.

AI-assisted outputs are reviewed rather than accepted automatically. Product strategy, problem framing, prioritization, requirements, risk decisions, evaluation criteria, and final product decisions remain human-owned.

This approach reflects a core principle of the portfolio: AI should augment product judgment rather than replace it.
