# ProductTrace — First Live Discovery Case

## Source

Discovery record:

`discovery-1790095378982-0`

Source Market Opportunity:

`market-opportunity-ai-1790082057133`

The opportunity entered Discovery after the PM modified the original AI proposal into:

> Evidence-backed signal detection that continuously identifies recurring customer and market themes, connects them to supporting evidence, and surfaces them to Product Managers for prioritization and opportunity decisions.

## Inherited evidence

The Discovery record preserved:

- original AI proposal
- PM-modified opportunity wording
- 7 supporting claims
- 4 internal evidence records
- 4 external market evidence records
- 2 governed Market Themes
- 3 assumptions
- 3 risks
- research brief lineage

Unsupported discovery fields were deliberately left blank.

## Initial readiness

Initial blockers included:
- Root Cause
- User Identity
- Expected User Outcome
- Value / Impact
- Learning / Measurement Outcome
- Feasibility / Dependency detail
- Falsification Criteria

Evidence sufficiency and assumption exposure were already sufficient.

## Grounded discovery updates

ProductTrace later added only evidence-supported or explicitly labeled content.

### Observed

Internal evidence captured:
- filters resetting during review
- approvals being buried in weekly digests
- unexplained large-file failure
- slower activity history after sustained usage

### Inferred

- product-team members responsible for reviewing customer signals, organizing insights, and prioritizing opportunities
- expected outcome around recurring-signal recognition, evidence retention, timely-action visibility, and evidence-backed prioritization
- dependency on access to customer-signal sources
- dependency on durable links between themes and evidence

### Assumption

- feedback volume is sufficient for useful recurring-theme detection
- urgency / theme classification can operate with acceptable error
- evidence linkage can improve prioritization usefulness or confidence
- urgent items can be surfaced without creating notification fatigue

## Discovery Plan

The PM-defined plan focuses on:

### Root Cause Validation
Methods:
- semi-structured interviews
- workflow observation
- review of additional support / product evidence

### Value / Impact Validation
Methods:
- interviews
- workflow observation
- evidence review of delays, missed approvals, rework, and context-loss consequences

### Learning Method
PM-defined / Unvalidated:

> Semi-structured user interviews, workflow observation, and concept/prototype testing focused on whether evidence-linked theme detection improves recurring-signal recognition, context retention, timely-action visibility, and prioritization confidence.

## PM Discovery Decision

Decision:

**More Discovery Needed**

Decision ID:

`discovery-decision-1790097462715`

Rationale:

The opportunity has sufficient evidence, user clarity, assumptions, constraints, falsification criteria, and a defined learning method, but Root Cause and Value / Impact remain unvalidated. Proceeding would turn assumptions into product commitment.

## Why this decision matters

The case demonstrates that ProductTrace does not reward a project for merely having AI-generated evidence.

The workflow stopped because two substantive discovery questions remained unanswered:

- What is the validated root cause?
- What actual user / business value would result from solving it?

This is intentional product governance.
